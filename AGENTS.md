# Agent notes

## Rules

- No code comments. A survivor states a why the code cannot show.
- The history of a codebase should not be encoded in or inferrable from the codebase itself. Systems at rest should look as if they were designed this way from the start. Exceptions exist, including git history and changelogs. Implications include immediate refactors to simplify and remove old patterns, and immediate sweeps and cleanups of code and documentation included with each modification.
- Changes land directly on `main`.
- `test-map.json` maps every touched path to the commands that must be green before landing.
- Sprites are drawn in code (`src/sprite.rs`). Any added asset is self-made or CC0 with its attribution recorded here, and stored lossless.
- `web/models.js` loads its models from their CDNs at runtime, each pinned there: MediaPipe Tasks Vision (`@mediapipe/tasks-vision`, Apache-2.0) with its BlazeFace short-range face detector and selfie segmenter models (Apache-2.0); ONNX Runtime Web (`onnxruntime-web`, MIT) with Depth Anything V2 Small (`onnx-community/depth-anything-v2-small`, int8, Apache-2.0, pinned to a Hugging Face revision). The person segmenter adds about 250 KB to the shared MediaPipe runtime; depth adds about 33 MB, fetched once the first empty scene is captured.

## Layout

- `src/seg.rs`: background capture (per-pixel median of a few frames plus a per-pixel chroma tolerance from their spread), then per-frame classification: a pixel is solid on a chroma shift beyond its tolerance or a luma ratio outside the shadow/highlight band, after dividing out a per-channel global gain (the median ratio per channel), which absorbs exposure and white-balance drift. A texture cue catches objects the colour of what they cover: how far log luma strays from its local mean, compared with its captured mean within a tolerance from the capture spread. A soft shadow edge is a smooth ramp and leaves it unchanged; a hard one is a line a few cells wide, so the cue must hold over a 7x7 area. Pixels judged background by both cues slowly update the colour model. A pixel turns solid after two foreground frames in a row and clears after three background ones, a 3x3 opening removes thin lines and a 3x3 closing seals hairline seams, then solid regions under 30 cells are dropped and enclosed gaps under 50 cells, too small for the bird, are filled. Pure Rust, no Bevy.
- `src/body.rs`: the bird's body against the mask, in mask cells. Moves at most one cell per sub-step on each axis, so it cannot tunnel. A solid that overlaps it lifts it to the nearest free row above. Small steps are climbed. Falling off the bottom respawns it at the top.
- `src/air.rs`: breadth-first flood fill of open cells from the bird. The bird breathes when its pocket reaches the screen edge; the same distance field steers monsters.
- `src/bird.rs`: the bird's brain. Picks perches by threat, novelty of the ground, and air, steers the body, and chooses its pose and expression.
- `src/baddie.rs`: a monster walks the distance field downhill to the bird, or straight at it when sealed out, never entering solid.
- `src/play.rs`: one round: air meter, monster waves, swats (solid that appeared within a quarter second next to a monster), loss.
- `src/sprite.rs`: pixel-art images.
- `web/models.js`: three models, each in its own module worker so the frame never waits for one; each worker takes a new frame only once it has answered the last. The page spawns the face detector; the vision worker below spawns the person segmenter and depth estimator as nested workers.
  - Face detection, about ten times a second, on a bitmap copied from the video. It returns the midpoint of the eyes; `src/webcam.rs` maps it through the same mirror and crop as the mask, and the bird treats it as a place it likes to be.
  - Person segmentation on a bitmap of the vision worker's mask-resolution canvas. Its latest mask, held until the next arrives, joins the colour and texture cues in `src/seg.rs`: a person is solid against any background, and the cells it covers are left out of the global gain estimate.
  - Monocular depth, at most five times a second, WebGPU where available and WebAssembly otherwise, on an input of about 43,000 pixels (238x182 for a 4:3 mask) in the mask's aspect, each side a multiple of the model's 14-pixel patch. If the fastest of the first three estimates takes over a second, the worker is ended and play continues without depth: on such a device it costs heat and battery for estimates too stale to help. Its output shifts with exposure, so `src/seg.rs` has the empty scene measured again, rendered from the background model under the current gain, after each capture and whenever the gain moves. It fits scale and shift between the frame's depth and the empty scene's on cells the other cues call background, and marks cells clearly nearer than the empty scene solid, but only where the picture has not changed since the depth was measured, so a slow estimate leaves no ghost of something that moved.
- `src/game.rs`, `src/webcam.rs`, `web/camera.js`: the wasm32-only Bevy front end. `getUserMedia` feeds the page's `<video>`, which CSS shows mirrored behind a transparent Bevy canvas; the game draws only the mask tint, the air pocket, the bird and the monsters. The mask has about 240x180 cells in the screen's aspect, taken when play starts and again when the screen turns between portrait and landscape (a phone turned, which moves the camera, so the empty scene is recaptured); other resizes keep the mask, letterboxed. `#stage` in `web/index.html`, sized by the `--aspect` the game sets, and the camera's `ScalingMode::AutoMin` both fit the mask's rectangle inside the screen, so they line up. When the countdown ends, `web/camera.js` locks exposure, white balance and focus at their current values wherever the browser exposes them as manual modes, before the empty scene is captured; recapture hands them back to the camera.
- `web/vision.js`, `src/vision.rs`: segmentation runs in a module worker, so the page's main thread only plays and draws; under 4x CPU throttling that took the frame rate from 17 to 40 fps. On each new video frame (`requestVideoFrameCallback`) the page transfers an `ImageBitmap` of it, with the centre crop, the mask size and a capture epoch. The worker draws it mirrored at mask resolution with medium smoothing, feeds the person and depth models, and runs `src/seg.rs` through `src/vision.rs`, built from the library crate as its own small wasm module (`segmenter.js` in `web/dist`; the game's binary target is named `game` so the two `.wasm` files do not collide). It answers with the mask, and the page drops answers for an older size or epoch. Like the model workers, it takes a new frame only once it has answered the last.

Native `cargo test` covers segmentation (busy background, exposure and white-balance drift, hard and soft cast shadows, a textured object the colour of the wall, moving and still objects, one-frame and tiny specks, holes, a person seen by the model, depth that differs only by scale and shift, something nearer by depth alone, stale depth), collision, air, monster pathing, rounds and sprites. Bevy is a wasm32-only dependency, so native builds stay small.

## Landing contract

A landing on `main` is complete only when the `pages` workflow (native tests, then the wasm build and deploy) is green on that sha. The deployed page is whatever `main` last built: https://bddap-bot.github.io/perch/

## End-to-end check without a webcam

Chromium can play a synthetic `.y4m` as the camera: `--use-fake-ui-for-media-stream --use-fake-device-for-media-stream --use-file-for-fake-video-capture=<file>.y4m`. Make the clip open on an empty scene that lasts past the countdown, then bring objects in. Headless WebGL2 through SwiftShader (`--use-angle=swiftshader-webgl --enable-unsafe-swiftshader`) renders it.
